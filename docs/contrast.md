# Contrast: design-system primitives, direction 2

Every text / background pair and every UI boundary used by `src/components/ui/**`, with its measured ratio.
Generated by `node _qa/ui/contrast.mjs` on 7 October 2026. Do not edit by hand: change the tokens or the component, then run it again.

- **Computed, not estimated.** Flat pairs are the WCAG 2.x relative-luminance formula on the hex values parsed from
  `src/styles/tokens.css` and `src/styles/base.css`. A translucent colour (`color-mix(…, transparent)`) is composited
  over the ground it sits on first. Ratios are truncated to two decimals, never rounded up.
- **Thresholds.** Text needs **4.5:1** (WCAG 1.4.3). Every flat text pair is held to that, including the large display
  type, so nothing on a flat ground relies on the 3:1 large-text allowance. Boundaries and marks a guest needs in order to
  find or operate a control need **3:1** (WCAG 1.4.11). Decoration and disabled controls have no requirement and are
  listed so the choice is visible.
- **Result, flat grounds: 104 pairs, 87 with a requirement, 0 failing.**
- **Result, text over photographs: 102 measurements, 0 failing** (method and tables in the last section).

How the primitives choose colour: they read the semantic tokens (`--text`, `--text-muted`, `--accent`, `--rule`,
`--surface`, `--focus`), which the helpers `.on-sand`, `.on-white`, `.on-twilight`, `.on-forest` and `.on-photo` re-point.
A light panel inside a dark band (Panel, Drawer, Dialog, AtAGlance) restates the light tokens and carries
`data-ground="light"`, so it produces the pairs of the white or sand group wherever it is placed.
The living reference is `/th/styleguide` and `/en/styleguide`, which draws the focus ring permanently on each ground.

## Text over photographs

Text over a photograph has no single background, so it is measured against the **lightest pixels behind it**.

**How `<Band scrim="hero">` protects text.** Three things are stacked over the photograph:
`--scrim-bottom` (the token), a pool of the twilight colour in the lower left corner where the words sit
(78% at the corner, 68% at 38% of its radius, 54% at 64%, gone at the edge; it is 110% of the band wide and 130% tall from 48rem,
170% by 125% on phones), and a **halo** in the same colour under every glyph (`text-shadow`: a 1px edge, a glow of a
quarter em capped at 5px, a glow of 0.9em capped at 28px). The pool leaves the upper right of the picture untouched,
which is what keeps the photograph bright; the halo is what carries small text over a pale sky.

**Two measurements, both from real pixels.**

1. *Scrim only, whole box.* The encoded derivative in `public/media` (the 1920px WebP) is laid out with sharp exactly
   as the browser lays it out (object-fit: cover around the catalogued focal point), the scrim gradients are composited
   on it per pixel, and the lightest 3 x 3 mean anywhere inside the text's box is taken. No halo. This is the most
   pessimistic figure: the lightest pixel in the box is often not behind a letter at all.
   The simulation was checked against captures of the page with the halo and the grain switched off: over 40 boxes
   the two differ by at most 8.2%.
2. *As rendered, behind the glyphs.* The page is captured twice in Chromium, once as it is and once with the glyphs made
   transparent (shadows stay). The pixels that differ are the glyphs; the second capture at those pixels is exactly what
   lies behind them: photograph, scrim, grain and halo. The lightest 3 x 3 mean of those is the background.

The verdict uses the second figure, because it is what the guest's eye is given, and it is held to **4.5:1 for all small
text** and to the large-text threshold of **3:1 for display type** (the hero line, a statement, an h1), though every display
line here also clears 4.5:1 as rendered. Where the first figure is below the threshold the row is marked: there the halo
is doing the work, and a hero built without it (the token scrims alone) would fail.

### What the photograph numbers say

- As rendered, the least small text gets over any of the four hero frames is **5.19:1**, and the least the hero line
  gets is **6.68:1**.
- With the scrim alone the lowest figure is **2.74:1**, and 6 of 80 rows are below their threshold. They are
  the eyebrow and the upper line of the hero sentence over the pale sky of 01_24, the clouds of 02_08 and 04_24. A hero
  that sets text with only `--scrim-top` and `--scrim-bottom` (ART-DIRECTION section 5 as written) will not reach 4.5:1
  for its eyebrow on those frames: `--scrim-bottom` is already at zero at 78% of the band's height, and a two-line hero
  sentence starts above that. Use `<Band scrim="hero">`, or the same pool and halo.
- Kickers and meta lines over a photograph are set in full paper by `<Band>`, not in the muted tone of `.on-photo`
  (#e4ded2): they are the smallest text on the picture.
- Outlines over a photograph (ghost button, chips) are paper at 58 to 72% and cannot be given one number. Each such
  control is identified by its label, which is measured above; fact chips also take a 38% veil of the twilight colour.
- A new frame under words should be run through this script before it ships: add its legacy id to `HERO_FRAMES` in
  `_qa/ui/photo-render.mjs`.
